What does it cost to foster a dog in the Rio Grande Valley?

You've seen the dog by the gas station for a week now. You want to help, but one question keeps stopping you: what will it cost? Here's an honest look at fostering a street dog in the Rio Grande Valley, using prices posted by Valley shelters and clinics.

Two ways to foster

How much you spend depends a lot on which road you take.

  • Foster for a shelter or rescue. The group takes the dog in, and you care for it at home until it's adopted or moved to a rescue. Costs are usually covered.
  • Take in a dog you found. You pick up the stray yourself and care for it on your own. Here the bills are yours, at least until a rescue can help.

Fostering through a shelter

When Palm Valley Animal Society in Edinburg put out a call for foster homes in January 2026, the shelter said it would provide everything foster parents need, including food and medication if the animal needs it. It also said there are no qualifications beyond being 18 years old.

To sign up with Palm Valley, fill out its dog foster questionnaire on pvastx.org. You can also email foster@pvastx.org or text (956) 278-0702 with questions. The shelter posts free foster guides on its site, covering mange, parvo, heartworm, crate training, and caring for mother dogs and puppies.

In Harlingen, the RGV Humane Society lists the pets that most need fosters, including tiny puppies, mother dogs with litters, long-stay dogs, and dogs with medical needs. Call (956) 556-5885 or visit 18038 Garrett Rd to ask how it works. For other groups across the Valley, see our list of local rescues.

Taking in a dog you found: a basic first visit

If you go it alone, low-cost clinics keep the basics within reach. Here's what a healthy adult dog's first round of care would cost at the RGV Humane Society's monthly community clinic in Harlingen, using its posted prices:

  • Rabies vaccine: $13 (plus $11 for the metal tag, which is sold separately)
  • DHPPV vaccine (distemper, parvo, and more): $16
  • Dewormer: $16
  • Heartworm test: $20
  • Microchip: $11
  • One month of flea and tick prevention: $26
  • One month of heartworm prevention: $7

That comes to about $120 for vaccines, a microchip, a heartworm test, and a month of prevention. Pre-registration is required, the dog has to come with you, and dogs must ride inside the vehicle, not in the truck bed. The heartworm pill also requires a printed negative heartworm test from the past 12 months.

Keep in mind these clinics handle routine care, not sick dogs. A dog with bad mange, an injury, or signs of parvo needs a full-service vet, and that can cost far more. Our clinics page lists upcoming low-cost events.

Spay or neuter

Fixing the dog is the step that stops the next litter from ending up on the street. Two Valley options with posted prices:

  • Palm Valley Animal Society, 2451 N US Expressway 281, Edinburg: dogs start at $100. Registration opens at 10 AM on the 1st of each month and often fills within the hour.
  • RGV Humane Society, Harlingen: $175 for a spay or neuter. Some flat-faced breeds, like pugs and bulldogs, aren't accepted.

Add that to the first visit and a found dog's basic care lands somewhere around $220 to $295, before food, a leash, or any treatment for illness.

Before you start

  1. Check for a microchip at a shelter or vet. The dog may have a family looking for it.
  2. Call a rescue or shelter first. Even if they're full, they may help with costs or advice. Our who to call page has numbers by city.
  3. Keep every receipt. Rescues and anyone helping you will want to see where the money went.
